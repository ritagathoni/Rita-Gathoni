Evaluation systems / business logic

Alibaba / Qwen

A demanding piece of work focused on whether model responses, business rules and evaluation logic were actually aligned. In this project, the answer was sometimes less interesting than the system judging it.

What it provesSystems thinking

I can identify when a model is wrong, but also when the evaluation logic is the real problem.

01 / Context

The assignment

The project involved reviewing Qwen outputs against a multi-check verifier framework tied to business scenarios. The work included dates, ETA changes, approval logic, conditional reasoning and structured outputs. In practice, that meant checking not only what the model said, but how each conclusion had been reached and whether the scoring logic behind it made sense.

02 / What I actually did

Evidence before verdict

My reviews involved tracing the response back to source facts, checking calculations, testing whether the answer matched the stated rules and documenting where assumptions had been introduced. When the model output and the verifier disagreed, I had to work out whether the model was at fault or whether the verification framework itself had a gap.

Supplied project materials reference a reviewed 19-check framework that moved from 9 of 19 aligned checks to 19 of 19 aligned checks after correction. I present that as a framework improvement within the reviewed materials, not as a company-wide metric.
03 / Why it was more complex than it looks

The judge can be wrong too

A weaker evaluator might stop at “pass” or “fail”. This project demanded more. Some responses looked polished while quietly smuggling in unsupported assumptions. Others were substantively right, but penalised by an incomplete verifier rule. That is the kind of distinction that matters in serious evaluation work.

  • Business-rule validation and approval-path logic
  • Source grounding and deterministic checks
  • Metadata alignment and output-format requirements
  • Root-cause analysis across model, prompt and verifier
04 / What the work demonstrates

The value of this case

This project shows a higher-order evaluation skill: not just grading an answer, but diagnosing the entire evaluation setup around it. It is the strongest example in my portfolio of why my work is not only about content quality. It is also about system integrity.

Keeping claims defensible
