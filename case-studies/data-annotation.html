01 / THE PROBLEM

The problem

Useful AI systems depend on reliable annotations and review signals. Poorly defined labels, inconsistent edge-case interpretation and missing evidence can undermine training data before the model is ever deployed.

  • Instruction following
  • Annotation guidelines
  • Quality and coverage
02 / WHAT I CONTRIBUTED

What I contributed

My broader AI work includes classification and quality review of generated text, preference ranking, structured output checks and human-in-the-loop review. I documented reasons for selections and escalated unclear cases rather than inventing rules.

  • Comparative ranking and label justification
  • Consistency checks and review calibration
  • Sensitive content and policy awareness
03 / QUALITY OPERATIONS

Quality operations

I worked with high-volume review processes and changing guidelines, applying quality criteria consistently, documenting uncertainty and translating repeat defects into useful feedback. The strongest signal is not a fabricated company-wide improvement statistic: it is the repeatability of the review method.

  • Error taxonomies and exception notes
  • Cross-reviewer calibration
  • Feedback loops for recurring model failures
04 / TRANSFERABLE TECHNICAL SKILLS

Transferable technical skills

The work connects to JSON and schema validation, metadata alignment, basic Python/SQL data investigation, spreadsheet analysis and audit-style record keeping. The result is a practice that covers both semantic quality and structural validity.

  • Data integrity
  • Structured records
  • Metadata and schema checks
